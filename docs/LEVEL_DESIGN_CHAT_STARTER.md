**Design direction**

- Create a journey through a believable environment. The route should weave through and be surrounded by the setting, not run as an obstacle strip beside a decoration strip.
- Build readable gameplay, a distinct identity for each section, a surrounding landscape and a visible destination.
- Use natural clusters, varied silhouettes, uneven spacing, different sizes and deliberate clearings. Avoid tree rows, identical arches and repeated stretched oval primitives.
- Think big. Trees should tower over avatars, with occasional gigantic landmarks. Use medium props and grounded micro-details as well. Do not confuse tiny clutter with immersion.
- Place plants on believable soil and appropriate biomes. Trees can participate in a designed course, but do not populate a bare cliff-jump area like a garden or obstruct landings.
- Make gaps require real jumps. Use bounded, seeded variation in platform position, height, width and angle. Keep a reachable route and allow multiplayer flow through safe areas and refuges.
- Give ledges structural support and exposed sections convincing depth. Hide the visible bottom in local haze; keep recovery behavior independent of visual depth.
- Design both mountain-facing and valley-facing views. Block mountain shortcuts. Preserve sightlines to the destination.
- Keep the ending clear and satisfying. No messy roof, props or cover hiding the finish.

**Mechanics**

- Hazards must feel natural and remain fair: clear red warnings, bounded random timing/size, useful cover or escape, believable sources and clean resets.
- Use visible rotation and arcs where appropriate. Do not fake bounces in midair. Controlled server motion is fine when safer and more predictable than free physics; describe it honestly.
- For moving logs, design the entire travel volume and lifecycle. Keep logs off land, banks complete, gaps meaningful and water/current motion coherent. Validate standing, walking, jumping and hidden reset separately; log motion alone does not prove rider behavior.
- Preserve shared race, checkpoint, staging and recovery systems. Do not introduce unrelated systems while doing level design.

**Art and performance**

- Use Blender as the main authoring tool for custom visible course geometry, with suitable project/Toolbox reuse. Moderate detail is welcome; do not arbitrarily restrict everything to crude low-poly forms.
- Spend geometry on player-visible course detail and immersion; simplify distant or hidden scenery. The owner's September 8 "smoke and mirrors" direction makes the player-height journey the visual acceptance target, not the whole-map overhead view.
- Prefer native atmosphere and distant geometry for map-wide haze. Keep particles local and purposeful. Avoid many large overlapping smoke sprites and unneeded hidden geometry.
- Review imported assets in Roblox at avatar scale. Check grounding, clipping, travel clearance and the finish reveal.
- Do not claim FPS gains from part counts or final playability from screenshots.

**How to work with me**

- Begin with relevant source and Studio inspection. Preserve accepted work and manual edits.
- Use a compact section plan: theme, player action, hazard, safe point, landmark and transition. Infer routine choices; ask only for missing information that materially affects the result.
- Use repository tools, Rojo and Studio MCP first. Minimize Computer Use. Use Blender without resetting unrelated open scenes.
- Spend most effort on the actual level design. Run proportional build/geometry checks and a short targeted gameplay check where needed. I own full playtests. Do not repeatedly traverse the course automatically.
- Follow applicable project approval rules. Prepare a concrete candidate before requesting a required asset upload or risky replacement approval. Keep backups. Never publish unless I explicitly ask.
- Keep scripts durable in Git/Rojo, identify the authored Studio model, and preserve manual edits before regenerating anything.
- Report what changed, what was checked and what remains uncertain in plain English. Stop once the requested work passes; do not expand into unrelated polishing or new features.
